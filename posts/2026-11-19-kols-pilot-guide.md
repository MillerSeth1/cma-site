Nogales International Airport (KOLS) sits about 7 miles northeast of downtown Nogales, Arizona, in Santa Cruz County, at the foot of the Santa Cruz Valley. The field lies just north of the U.S.–Mexico border along State Route 82. It is a non-towered Santa Cruz County airport, an international airport of entry, and a working gateway for pilots flying the southern Arizona border corridor.

This guide collects airfield, frequency, history, and arrival information for KOLS.

## Where the field sits

KOLS occupies land the Santa Cruz County Chamber of Commerce bought in 1928 — the same property the airport uses today — and the county still owns it. From the air, the field is a high-desert strip with mountains around it and the international line only a short hop to the south.

Surveyed field elevation is **3,955 ft MSL**, with a magnetic variation of 9° E. At that elevation, density altitude is part of everyday flying here, especially on warmer days, and it affects takeoff performance, climb, and true airspeed. Compute density altitude from the current altimeter and temperature before departure, and use your aircraft's performance data. The on-field ASOS (121.125, or 520-287-9332 by phone) is the place to start for official observations.

## A southern Arizona border gateway

KOLS is distinctive because of where it sits and how long it has served this corridor. Aviation reached Nogales even before the present airport existed: U.S. Army troops were at Nogales in November 1910, and Camp Stephen D. Little later included an aviation field — the earliest documented flying field in the community. That military field stood at the north edge of the Nogales townsite. It was **not** today's airport, which is 7 to 8 miles northeast of downtown on State Route 82.

Today's field opened in **March 1928** as a graded native-earth and grass rectangle, 1,980 by 4,180 feet, after the Chamber of Commerce bought the land that January. The property was deeded to Santa Cruz County on **October 3, 1928**, and the airport was officially dedicated in September 1929.

By **1937**, the Bureau of Air Commerce already listed Nogales as an **international airport of entry**, then a gravel field. Nogales Municipal Airport appears on the 1946 U.S. Code list of airports of entry, and Nogales International Airport is still designated today under **19 CFR § 122.13**, with an on-field U.S. Customs and Border Protection port of entry.

Federally funded construction on **March 31, 1944**, gave the airport its first paved runways, including a new 6,000-foot northeast–southwest asphalt runway that is the backbone of today's Runway 04/22. (That runway was numbered 3/21 for most of its history.) Brief airline service followed: Arizona Airways flew DC-3s through Nogales from 1946 until March 1, 1948, and Frontier Airlines began DC-3 service on the Phoenix–Tucson–Nogales–Bisbee–Douglas route on June 1, 1950. That temporary route authority was not renewed in 1953.

The modern runway took its present length in **1996**, when a 1,200-foot extension stretched the 1944 pavement to today's **7,200 × 100 feet**. An on-field VOR/DME dates to 1975. Automated ASOS weather observations have been on record since at least 1999, and 2022 state grants funded a new LED rotating beacon, runway end identifier lights, runway edge lights, guidance signs, and a new visual approach guidance system.

The [full KOLS history](../about.html#history) on this site walks through that record in more detail, with sources.

## Arriving from Mexico: airport of entry

FAA data lists KOLS as an international airport of entry. If you are arriving from Mexico or another foreign point, plan U.S. Customs and Border Protection requirements well ahead of your flight, and treat official CBP guidance as controlling.

CBP's [General Aviation Airport Fact Sheet for KOLS](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/KOLS%20GA%20Airport%20Fact%20Sheet%2020260224.pdf) (dated February 24, 2026) asks pilots to secure permission to land by contacting CBP before departing the foreign airport, and at least one hour before the expected arrival at KOLS. Permission is granted with a tolerance of 30 minutes either side of the ETA; if your arrival falls outside that window, you must contact CBP again. Park on the ramp at the Federal Inspection Station on the east side of the terminal, and be ready to present passports, visas, pilot certificate, medical certificate, aircraft registration and user fee decal. CBP office hours are 0800–1600 Mountain Time daily, and processing can be arranged 24/7 with at least one hour's notice: call 520-287-1433 during those hours, or 520-375-5785 after hours. The fact sheet notes a maximum passenger count of 20 at this location.

Always follow current CBP instructions, NOTAMs, and the Chart Supplement. This summary is not a substitute for official guidance.
